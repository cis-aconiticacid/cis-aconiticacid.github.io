I’m Xinyang Wen (文心扬), an undergraduate at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, double-majoring in Mathematics and Computer Science, and graduating in Spring 2027.

I work on fast numerical methods for machine learning, especially entropic optimal transport on GPUs, and on decoding visual information from EEG. I like problems where a clean piece of structure (a cone, a spectrum, a contraction rate) explains why an algorithm is fast in practice, and then turning that explanation into code that runs well.

Research

DrainSinkhorn: batched entropic optimal transport on GPUs

DrainSinkhorn: Instance-Resident Batched Entropic Optimal Transport on GPUs. Xinyang Wen. arXiv:2607.24741 · code

DrainSinkhorn solves thousands of independent entropic OT problems in one batch. Each problem retires on its own once a two-sided marginal L1 check passes, instead of waiting for the slowest one in the batch, and a Triton “resident” kernel keeps small problems (N, M ≤ 64) on-chip. Benchmarks cover real tracking (MOT17) and domain-decomposition workloads against POT and FlashSinkhorn.

Decoding images from EEG

Ongoing. Retrieving the image a person saw from a single EEG trial, scored by nearest-neighbour search in a DINO embedding space (THINGS-EEG2). My focus is cross-subject alignment: per-subject encoders trained to map into a shared space, then a subject-agnostic encoder that works on people it hasn’t seen. I’m also looking at shortcuts in EEG benchmarks that let models score well without decoding the stimulus.

Clip identity in cross-subject emotion evaluation on SEED-VII

Completed; blog post. Tests 10 published emotion-recognition models on SEED-VII. Each model with a high reported score has its own route to the label that bypasses emotion: recognizing the clip from recording length, recognizing it from a single time window of EEG, or writing the label into its input features. Models without such a route stay between 20% and 30% accuracy, where chance for 7 classes is 14.3%. None of the 152 papers checked from 2018 to 2026 reports this problem, and no cross-subject result on SEED-VII is clip-disjoint.

Frequency-domain convolution and its equivalence to original-domain Hadamard products

Completed; blog post. Studies the equivalence between networks that apply an FFT and then convolve the FFT coefficients along the frequency axis (such as FFC and LaMa) and networks built from Hadamard products in the original domain. The linear part is exactly a windowing in the original domain; polynomial substitutes for ReLU-type activations have an error bound that can be derived in advance, but the bound is too loose on real models to be usable in practice. Keeping the activation in the frequency domain and replacing only the convolution with original-domain windowing plus a pair of FFTs is exact for any pointwise activation, and needs 2.23 times fewer multiplications on the experimental network.
